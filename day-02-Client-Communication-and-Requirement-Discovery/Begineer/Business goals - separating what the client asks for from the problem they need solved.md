# Business Goals: Understanding What the Client Wants vs. What the Business Actually Needs

## What Are Business Goals?

Business goals are the results a company wants to achieve through a project. These could include increasing revenue, reducing costs, saving time, improving customer experience, or making business operations more efficient.

When working on a software project, it is important to understand **why the client wants something, not just what they want to build.**

For example, a client might ask for a mobile application, but their actual goal might be to make ordering easier for customers and reduce the workload on employees. The application is just one possible solution to that problem.

## Understanding What the Client Asks For vs. What They Actually Need

Clients often come to a project with a solution already in mind. However, that solution may not be the best way to solve their problem.

For example, a client might say they need a mobile application for their business. After asking a few questions, the project manager discovers that:

- Customers currently place orders through phone calls.
- Employees manually record every order.
- Some orders get missed or recorded incorrectly.
- Customers frequently call to check their order status.

Now the actual problem is much clearer. The business needs a better way to manage orders and keep customers updated. A mobile application might help, but it is important to understand the requirements before deciding on the solution.

| What the client asks for | What they might actually need |
|---|---|
| A mobile application | Easier access to services |
| An admin dashboard | Better control over business operations |
| Automated emails | Faster customer communication |
| A new website design | Better user experience and more sales |
| An employee management system | Less manual HR work |
| An online payment system | A simpler payment process |

## How to Identify the Actual Business Problem

A project manager should not immediately agree to every solution a client suggests. Instead, they should understand the problem behind the request.

There are a few ways to do this:

### 1. Ask Why Questions

Keep asking why until you understand the reason behind the client's request.

**Example:**

- **Client:** We need automated email notifications.
- **Project Manager:** Why?
- **Client:** Customers frequently ask about their order status.
- **Project Manager:** Why can't they check it themselves?
- **Client:** Because we don't have an order-tracking feature.

Now the project manager understands the actual issue. Instead of simply building an email system, the team can consider whether an order-tracking feature would solve the problem more effectively.

### 2. Understand the Current Process

Before suggesting improvements, understand how the business currently handles the task.

Ask questions such as:

- How is this task currently performed?
- Who is responsible for it?
- How much time does it take?
- What difficulties do employees face?
- Where do mistakes usually happen?

This helps the team identify what needs to be improved, automated, or replaced.

### 3. Identify the Desired Outcome

Understanding the problem is not enough. The project manager also needs to know what the client expects to achieve.

Ask:

- What do you want to improve?
- What would a successful result look like?
- How would this solution benefit your business?
- How will you measure success?

This ensures that the proposed solution actually supports the client's business goals.

### 4. Find the Root Cause

Sometimes what the client describes is only a symptom of a deeper problem. The project manager needs to investigate what is actually causing the issue rather than focusing only on what the client notices.

## Understanding Symptoms, Problems, and Solutions

These three concepts are closely related but have different meanings.

| Concept | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Symptom | What the client notices. | Customers complain about delayed orders. |
| Problem | What is causing the issue. | Employees process orders manually. |
| Solution | What can fix the problem. | Automate order processing through an order management system. |

Understanding this difference helps the team avoid building solutions that only address the symptoms instead of fixing the actual problem.

## How to Document Business Goals

Once the project manager understands the client's needs, the findings should be documented. This ensures that everyone involved in the project understands why the project is being built and what it is expected to achieve.

A business goals document should include:

| Section | What to document |
|---|---|
| Client Request | What the client initially asked for. |
| Business Problem | The issue the business is experiencing. |
| Root Cause | Why the problem is happening. |
| Business Goals | What the client wants to achieve. |
| Proposed Solution | How the team plans to address the problem. |
| Success Criteria | How the team will measure whether the solution worked. |

**The main idea:** Don't just build what the client asks for. Understand why they are asking for it, identify the actual problem, and then propose a solution that helps the business achieve its goals.
