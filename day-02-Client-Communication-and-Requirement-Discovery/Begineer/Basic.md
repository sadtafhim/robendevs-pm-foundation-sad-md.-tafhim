
# Business Goals, Discovery Meetings, Meeting Notes, and Stakeholder Management

These topics help project managers understand what a client actually needs, gather project requirements, document important discussions, and identify the people responsible for project decisions and approvals.

## 1. Business Goals: Understanding What the Client Actually Needs

Business goals are the results a company wants to achieve through a project, such as increasing revenue, reducing costs, improving customer experience, or making operations more efficient.

A client may request a particular feature or system, but the project manager must understand the **business problem behind that request**.

For example, a client may request a mobile application, but their actual problem might be that employees manually process orders, causing delays and mistakes.

### How to Identify Actual Business Needs

1. **Ask Why Questions:** Understand why the client wants a particular feature or solution.
2. **Understand Current Processes:** Identify how the business currently works and where problems occur.
3. **Identify Desired Outcomes:** Understand what the client wants to achieve.
4. **Find the Root Cause:** Identify the actual reason behind the problem instead of focusing only on its symptoms.

### Symptoms vs. Problems vs. Solutions

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Symptom | What the client notices. |
| Problem | What is causing the issue. |
| Solution | What can fix the problem. |

### Documenting Business Goals

Record the following information:

- Client request.
- Actual business problem.
- Root cause.
- Business goals.
- Proposed solution.
- Success criteria.

The main idea is to understand *why* the client needs something before deciding *what* should be built.

## 2. Discovery Meetings

A discovery meeting is an initial meeting between the client and project team to understand the client's business, goals, problems, expectations, and project requirements.

### Why Are Discovery Meetings Important?

- Understand the client's actual needs.
- Identify business problems and requirements.
- Define project scope.
- Discuss budget and deadlines.
- Set expectations.
- Avoid misunderstandings.

### Preparing a Discovery Meeting Agenda

| Topic | What to Discuss |
|---|---|
| Introduction | Participants and meeting objectives. |
| Business Overview | Client's business, services, and customers. |
| Business Problems | Current challenges and issues. |
| Project Requirements | Required features and functionality. |
| Budget and Timeline | Expected cost, deadlines, and milestones. |
| Expectations | Deliverables, responsibilities, and next steps. |

### Asking Open-Ended Questions

Open-ended questions encourage clients to explain their needs instead of answering only yes or no.

Examples:

- What are your main business goals?
- What problems are you currently facing?
- How does your existing process work?
- What features would make the system useful?
- What would make this project successful?

Closed-ended questions are also useful for confirming specific details, but open-ended questions are better for exploring requirements.

### Active Listening

Active listening means paying attention to the client's answers, understanding their concerns, and confirming that you have understood correctly.

Important practices:

- Give your full attention.
- Avoid interrupting.
- Ask follow-up questions.
- Take notes.
- Confirm your understanding.
- Pay attention to concerns and priorities.

### After the Discovery Meeting

1. Organize meeting notes.
2. Identify missing information.
3. Prepare meeting minutes.
4. Share a meeting summary with the client.
5. Define next steps, such as preparing requirements, estimates, or a project proposal.

## 3. Meeting Notes and Minutes of Meeting (MoM)

Meeting notes are written records of important discussions, decisions, questions, and tasks covered during a meeting.

**Minutes of Meeting (MoM)** are structured documents that summarize what happened during a meeting and what needs to happen afterward.

### What Should MoM Include?

| Section | Information |
|---|---|
| Meeting Details | Date, time, participants, and title. |
| Agenda | Topics discussed. |
| Discussion Summary | Important points and updates. |
| Decisions | Agreements and conclusions. |
| Action Items | Tasks, responsible people, and deadlines. |
| Pending Questions | Issues requiring clarification. |
| Next Meeting | Details of the next meeting, if scheduled. |

### Decisions vs. Action Items

- **Decisions:** What has been agreed upon.
- **Action Items:** What needs to be done, who will do it, and when.

### How to Prepare and Share MoM

1. Take notes during the meeting.
2. Organize discussions, decisions, and tasks.
3. Confirm responsibilities and deadlines.
4. Prepare a clear MoM document.
5. Share it with relevant participants, ideally within 24 hours.
6. Follow up on assigned tasks.

### Where to Store Meeting Notes

Common tools include Google Docs, Notion, Confluence, Microsoft Teams, and Jira.

Meeting notes should be stored in a shared, organized location where authorized team members can access them.

## 4. Stakeholder Management

### What Are Stakeholders?

Stakeholders are individuals or groups who are involved in a project, influence its decisions, or are affected by its outcome.

They may include business owners, managers, employees, end users, and development teams.

Identifying stakeholders helps project managers understand who makes decisions, who uses the product, and who approves deliverables.

### Three Important Types of Client-Side Stakeholders

**1. Decision Makers**

People who have the authority to make important business and project decisions.

Responsibilities:
- Approve budgets.
- Set priorities.
- Make major project decisions.
- Approve scope changes.

**2. Users**

People who will actually use the final software or application.

Responsibilities:
- Explain their daily workflows.
- Identify problems and requirements.
- Provide feedback.
- Participate in testing.

**3. Approvers**

People responsible for reviewing and formally accepting project deliverables.

Responsibilities:
- Review completed work.
- Check whether requirements are satisfied.
- Approve designs, features, or final releases.

One person can have multiple roles, but their responsibilities and authority must be clearly defined.

### Stakeholder Register

A stakeholder register is a document that records important project stakeholders and their responsibilities.

It typically includes:

- Stakeholder name.
- Role and responsibilities.
- Decision-making authority.
- Approval authority.
- Communication requirements.

It helps the team know who to contact for different decisions, feedback, and approvals.

### Managing Stakeholders Effectively

- Clearly define everyone's responsibilities.
- Identify the primary client contact.
- Involve actual users during requirement gathering and testing.
- Establish who approves each deliverable.
- Document important decisions.
- Provide regular project updates.

## 5. RACI Matrix

RACI is a responsibility assignment framework that clarifies who is involved in completing different project activities.

RACI stands for:

| Role | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **R – Responsible** | Person who performs the actual work. |
| **A – Accountable** | Person ultimately responsible for the outcome. |
| **C – Consulted** | People who provide feedback or expertise. |
| **I – Informed** | People who need to receive updates. |

### Why Is RACI Important?

- Clarifies responsibilities.
- Prevents confusion and overlapping work.
- Identifies who makes final decisions.
- Improves communication and accountability.

## Key Takeaways

1. **Business Goals:** Understand the actual business problem instead of blindly following the client's requested solution.
2. **Discovery Meetings:** Gather business information, requirements, expectations, budget, and timeline before starting development.
3. **Active Listening:** Ask relevant questions, listen carefully, and confirm your understanding.
4. **Meeting Notes:** Document important discussions, decisions, action items, owners, and deadlines.
5. **Stakeholders:** Identify decision makers, users, and approvers early in the project.
6. **RACI:** Clearly define who is responsible, accountable, consulted, and informed.

**Remember:** A project manager must understand the client's business needs, gather accurate requirements, maintain clear documentation, and ensure everyone knows their responsibilities before and throughout the project.
