
# Written Confirmation: Confirming Agreements Through Email or Confluence

## 1. What Is Written Confirmation?

Written confirmation means recording important project discussions, decisions, and agreements so everyone has a clear understanding of what was agreed upon.

In software projects, discussions often happen during meetings or calls. However, relying only on verbal agreements can lead to misunderstandings.

For example, a client agrees to a delivery date of December 20 during a meeting. Later, they claim the deadline was December 10. Without written confirmation, it becomes difficult to resolve the disagreement.

A project manager should document important agreements through email or a shared platform such as Confluence.

## 2. Why Is Written Confirmation Important?

Written confirmation helps prevent misunderstandings and keeps everyone aligned throughout the project.

Its main purposes are to:

- **Prevent misunderstandings:** Everyone has the same understanding of what was agreed upon.
- **Create accountability:** Responsibilities and deadlines are clearly recorded.
- **Provide evidence:** There is a written record of decisions and approvals.
- **Track changes:** Everyone can see what changed and when.
- **Avoid disputes:** Both parties can refer to documented agreements instead of relying on memory.
- **Maintain project continuity:** Team members can review previous decisions when needed.

Important agreements to document include project scope, requirements, deadlines, budget, design approvals, change requests, and meeting decisions.

## 3. When Should You Use Email?

Email is best for direct communication with clients, stakeholders, and other team members.

Use email when you need to:

- Confirm project scope and requirements.
- Get approval for designs or deliverables.
- Confirm deadlines and budget.
- Discuss additional requests and their impact.
- Summarize important meeting decisions.
- Notify stakeholders about changes.

**Example:**

After a client meeting, the project manager sends an email summarizing the agreed features, deadlines, and budget. The client reviews the details and confirms that everything is correct.

This creates a written record that both parties can refer to later.

## 4. When Should You Use Confluence?

Confluence is a documentation platform where teams can organize and maintain project information in one central location.

Use Confluence to:

- Document project scope and requirements.
- Store meeting notes and decisions.
- Maintain project timelines and milestones.
- Track change requests.
- Record approvals.
- Maintain a history of important project decisions.

**Example:**

A client approves removing the wishlist feature from the first release. The project manager records the decision, its reason, the approval date, and its impact in Confluence.

Anyone working on the project can access this information without searching through old emails.

## 5. Email vs. Confluence

Both tools are useful, but they serve different purposes.

| Email | Confluence |
|---|---|
| Best for direct communication. | Best for organized documentation. |
| Used to confirm agreements and request approvals. | Used to maintain project records. |
| Useful for communicating with clients and stakeholders. | Useful for sharing information with the project team. |
| Keeps a history of email conversations. | Keeps project information in one central location. |
| Good for notifying people about changes. | Good for tracking decisions, requirements, and changes. |

### Should You Use Both?

Yes. They work best together.

For example:

1. The client requests an additional feature during a meeting.
2. The project manager evaluates its impact on cost and timeline.
3. The project manager emails the client with the details.
4. The client approves the change through email.
5. The project manager updates the project documentation in Confluence.
6. The development team is informed about the approved change.

This ensures that the agreement is confirmed with the client and recorded for the entire team.

## 6. Best Practices for Written Confirmation

- **Confirm important agreements quickly:** Send a summary after important meetings.
- **Use clear language:** Avoid vague statements about deadlines, costs, or responsibilities.
- **Record responsibilities:** Mention who is responsible for each task.
- **Include dates:** Record when decisions were made and when tasks are due.
- **Document changes:** Record changes to scope, budget, and timelines.
- **Keep documentation updated:** Make sure Confluence reflects the latest agreements.
- **Record approvals:** Clearly document who approved important decisions and changes.
