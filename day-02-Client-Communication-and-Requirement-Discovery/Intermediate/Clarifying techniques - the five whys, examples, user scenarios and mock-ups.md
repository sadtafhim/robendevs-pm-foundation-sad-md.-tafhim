# Requirement Clarification: Five Whys and Mock-ups

## 1. The Five Whys Technique

The Five Whys is a requirement clarification technique used to identify the root cause behind a client's request or problem. It involves repeatedly asking "Why?" to understand the actual business problem instead of immediately accepting the client's proposed solution.

**Example:**

A client requests an automated email notification system.

- **Why 1:** Why do you need automated emails?  
  Customers frequently contact the company to ask about their orders.

- **Why 2:** Why do customers need to contact you?  
  They cannot check their order status themselves.

- **Why 3:** Why can't they check their order status?  
  The website does not have an order-tracking feature.

- **Why 4:** Why doesn't the website have order tracking?  
  It was originally built only to display products and receive orders.

- **Why 5:** Why is order tracking needed now?  
  The number of orders has increased, and the support team spends too much time answering customer questions.

**Identified Problem:** The support team is overwhelmed with order-related inquiries because customers cannot track their purchases independently.

**Possible Solution:** Introduce an order-tracking feature along with automated email notifications.

The goal is not to ask exactly five questions but to continue asking until the actual problem is understood.

## 2. Mock-ups

A mock-up is a visual representation of how a website or application is expected to look before development begins. It shows design elements such as colors, images, typography, buttons, and the placement of different sections.

Mock-ups help clients visualize the proposed interface and provide feedback before developers start building it. This reduces design misunderstandings and avoids unnecessary changes later.

### Wireframe vs Mock-up

Both are used to visualize a proposed interface, but they serve different purposes.

| Wireframe | Mock-up |
|---|---|
| Shows the basic structure of a page. | Shows how the finished interface is expected to look. |
| Focuses on layout and element placement. | Focuses on colors, typography, images, and styling. |
| Usually simple and low-detail. | Usually detailed and visually refined. |
| Helps plan where elements should go. | Helps review and finalize the visual design. |
