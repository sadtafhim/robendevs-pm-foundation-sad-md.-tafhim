
# Daily Standups, Jira Boards, Status Reporting, Sprint Reviews, and Retrospectives

These practices help Agile teams track progress, communicate updates, identify blockers, and improve their workflow.

## 1. Daily Standups

### What Is a Daily Standup?

A daily standup is a short meeting held every working day to discuss progress, coordinate tasks, and identify blockers.

In Scrum, it is called the **Daily Scrum**. It should generally take no more than 15 minutes.

### The Three Main Questions

Each team member briefly answers:

1. **What did I do yesterday?** – Summarise completed work and progress.
2. **What will I do today?** – Explain current priorities.
3. **Are there any blockers?** – Identify anything preventing progress.

### How to Run a 15-Minute Standup

- **Minutes 0–2:** Review the sprint goal.
- **Minutes 2–10:** Team members provide updates.
- **Minutes 10–13:** Identify blockers and dependencies.
- **Minutes 13–15:** Confirm priorities and follow-up actions.

Detailed problem-solving should happen in separate discussions.

### Capturing Blockers

A blocker is anything preventing someone from completing their work.

Common blockers include:
- Missing requirements or design files.
- Delayed client feedback or approval.
- Technical issues.
- Dependencies on other teams.
- Missing system or tool access.

Each blocker should be documented with:

- **Description:** What is preventing progress?
- **Affected task:** Which Jira issue is affected?
- **Owner:** Who is responsible for resolving it?
- **Impact:** How does it affect delivery?
- **Status:** Open, in progress, or resolved.

The project manager or Scrum Master should follow up until blockers are resolved.

## 2. Jira Boards

### What Is a Jira Board?

A Jira board is a visual workspace for tracking tasks and monitoring their progress through different stages of development.

### Common Workflow Columns

| Column | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Backlog | Work that has not been scheduled. |
| To Do | Work selected for execution. |
| In Progress | Work currently being handled. |
| In Review | Work waiting for review or testing. |
| Done | Work that meets completion requirements. |

The workflow can vary depending on project requirements.

### Moving Issues Through the Workflow

Each task is represented by a Jira issue. Its status should be updated as work progresses.

Typical workflow:

1. A developer starts working and moves the issue to **In Progress**.
2. Development is completed, and the issue moves to **In Review**.
3. The reviewer checks the work.
4. If changes are needed, the issue returns to **In Progress**.
5. Once approved and completed, it moves to **Done**.

### Keeping Jira Status Accurate

Accurate statuses help teams:

- Track actual project progress.
- Understand task ownership.
- Identify delayed tasks.
- Monitor sprint progress.
- Provide reliable client updates.

### Best Practices

- Update statuses whenever work changes stages.
- Assign active tasks to responsible team members.
- Keep descriptions and acceptance criteria clear.
- Record important decisions and changes in comments.
- Link related tasks and dependencies.
- Review the board regularly for outdated statuses.

Jira should always reflect the actual state of the work.

## 3. Status Reporting

### What Is Status Reporting?

Status reporting is the process of communicating project progress, current activities, risks, and upcoming work to clients and stakeholders.

Weekly reports keep stakeholders informed without requiring them to attend internal meetings.

### What a Weekly Status Report Should Include

**1. Overall Project Status**

Indicate the current project condition:

- **On Track:** Progress follows the planned timeline.
- **At Risk:** Potential issues could affect delivery.
- **Delayed:** Work is behind schedule.

**2. Completed Work**

Summarise important tasks, features, and milestones completed during the reporting period.

**3. Current Work**

Explain which tasks and features are currently being developed or tested.

**4. Risks and Blockers**

Identify issues that could affect delivery, explain their impact, and mention the actions being taken.

**5. Next Steps**

List the main activities and priorities planned for the next reporting period.

### Best Practices

- Keep updates clear and factual.
- Focus on significant progress.
- Report risks and delays honestly.
- Include measurable progress where possible.
- Highlight actions required from the client.
- Maintain a consistent reporting format.

## 4. Sprint Review and Retrospective

Sprint reviews and retrospectives are meetings held near the end of a sprint. Both evaluate the previous sprint but focus on different areas.

- **Sprint Review:** Focuses on the product and completed work.
- **Sprint Retrospective:** Focuses on the team's process and improvements.

### A. Sprint Review

**What Is a Sprint Review?**

A sprint review is a meeting where the team presents completed work to stakeholders, collects feedback, and discusses future priorities.

**What Happens During a Sprint Review?**

1. **Review the Sprint Goal:** Explain what the sprint aimed to achieve.
2. **Demonstrate Completed Work:** Show completed features and functionality.
3. **Discuss Incomplete Work:** Explain unfinished tasks and their reasons.
4. **Collect Feedback:** Gather stakeholder comments and requested changes.
5. **Discuss Priorities:** Identify what should be prioritised in future sprints.

**Expected Outputs:**

- Feedback on completed features.
- Identified changes and improvements.
- Updated product backlog.
- Agreed priorities for upcoming work.

### B. Sprint Retrospective

**What Is a Sprint Retrospective?**

A sprint retrospective is a meeting where the team evaluates its workflow, communication, collaboration, and overall performance.

Its purpose is to identify problems and agree on improvements for the next sprint.

**What Happens During a Retrospective?**

1. **What Went Well?** Identify successful practices.
2. **What Did Not Go Well?** Discuss problems that affected the sprint.
3. **What Can Be Improved?** Identify practical solutions.
4. **Agree on Action Items:** Assign responsibilities for implementing improvements.

**Expected Outputs:**

- Identified strengths and weaknesses.
- Practical improvement ideas.
- Assigned action items.
- Changes to apply in the next sprint.

### Sprint Review vs. Sprint Retrospective

| Area | Sprint Review | Sprint Retrospective |
|---|---|---|
| Focus | Product and completed work | Team and workflow |
| Purpose | Inspect progress and collect feedback | Improve team processes |
| Participants | Scrum Team and stakeholders | Scrum Team |
| Discussion | Features and functionality | Communication and challenges |
| Output | Updated product backlog | Improvement action items |
| Main Question | Are we building the right product? | How can we work better? |

### Recommended Order

1. **Sprint Review:** Demonstrate completed work and collect feedback.
2. **Sprint Retrospective:** Evaluate the workflow and identify improvements.
3. **Next Sprint:** Apply the agreed improvements and continue development.

The sprint review improves the product, while the retrospective improves how the team works.
