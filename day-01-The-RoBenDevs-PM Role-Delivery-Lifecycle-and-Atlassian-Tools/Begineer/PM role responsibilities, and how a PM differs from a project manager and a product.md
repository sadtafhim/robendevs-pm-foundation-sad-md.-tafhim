# The Role of a PM

A Product Manager (PM) is responsible for deciding what product to build, why it should be built, who it is for, and how it creates value for both customers and the business.

## Key Responsibilities of a Product Manager

### 1. Defining Long-Term Product Strategy and Vision

A Product Manager defines the long-term vision of a product by understanding the market and competitive landscape. Based on this understanding, they define product goals and success criteria.

A Product Manager identifies business opportunities by analyzing market gaps and developing strategies for the growth and development of the product.

**Example:** Deciding that an e-commerce platform should focus on improving customer retention rather than simply acquiring more users.

### 2. Market and User Research

One of the main responsibilities of a Product Manager is to understand the market and its users.

They are responsible for identifying customer pain points, understanding how the market responds to those problems, and finding gaps that can be addressed through the product.

To achieve this, they:

- Conduct customer interviews and surveys.
- Analyze user behavior and feedback.
- Research competitors.
- Identify customer needs and market opportunities.

**Example:** Discovering that customers abandon their shopping carts because the checkout process is too complicated.

### 3. Product Roadmap Planning

A Product Manager's main responsibility is to manage the product's direction and determine what should be developed over time.

For this, they create a product roadmap.

A product roadmap is a high-level document that defines the product's future direction, including what features will be developed and when they are expected to be delivered.

To create a roadmap, a Product Manager:

- Defines which features should be developed.
- Prioritizes features based on business impact.
- Decides what should be built now versus later.
- Communicates the product roadmap to stakeholders for feedback.

### 4. Requirement Definition

A Product Manager defines product requirements through research, customer feedback, and client correspondence. They then communicate these requirements to stakeholders in the form of clear and actionable features.

For this purpose, they create Product Requirement Documents (PRDs).

A PRD is a document that contains the essential information about a product or feature, including:

- Product overview and objectives.
- Stakeholder information.
- Functional requirements and features.
- User stories.
- Acceptance criteria.

### 5. Cross-Functional Collaboration

A Product Manager is responsible for collaborating with different stakeholders across various departments, including Engineering, UI/UX Design, Marketing, Sales, Customer Support, and C-suite leadership.

| Team             | PM's Involvement                                       |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Engineering      | Explain product requirements and technical trade-offs. |
| UI/UX Design     | Define user problems and review proposed experiences.  |
| Marketing        | Plan product positioning and launch.                   |
| Sales            | Understand customer and market requirements.           |
| Customer Support | Analyze customer complaints and user feedback.         |
| Leadership       | Communicate product performance and strategy.          |

### 6. Product Performance and Analytics

A Product Manager is required to work with product-related data and metrics. This involves tracking product KPIs and making recommendations based on those metrics to improve product performance.

The PM must understand which metrics are important for the product and analyze them thoroughly.

This helps the Product Manager make informed decisions about the product and maintain transparent communication with product stakeholders.

### 7. Product Launch and Continuous Improvement

A Product Manager coordinates product launches and works with Marketing and Sales teams to execute the product's market launch.

After the launch, they monitor product performance and collect relevant data and customer feedback.

Based on the information gathered, they make informed decisions about further improvements to the product.

## Product Manager vs Project Manager vs Product Owner

The difference between a Product Manager, Project Manager, and Product Owner lies in their responsibilities and primary areas of focus.

### Product Manager

The Product Manager focuses on the question:

**"Are we building the right thing?"**

They focus on:

- Product value.
- Customer needs.
- Business strategy.
- Long-term product direction.

### Project Manager

The Project Manager focuses on the question:

**"Are we delivering it properly?"**

Their focus is on:

- Timeline.
- Budget.
- Resources.
- Risks.
- Dependencies.
- Project delivery.

### Product Owner

The Product Owner focuses on the question:

**"What should the development team build next?"**

Their focus is on:

- Product backlog.
- User stories.
- Prioritization.
- Development-team clarification.

### Detailed Comparison

| Area                | Product Manager              | Project Manager              | Product Owner                        |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Primary focus       | Product success              | Project delivery             | Backlog and team value               |
| Main concern        | What and why                 | When and how                 | What comes next                      |
| Strategy            | Defines product strategy     | Aligns project execution     | Supports product goals               |
| Roadmap             | Owns product roadmap         | Manages project plan         | Helps translate roadmap into backlog |
| Requirements        | Defines product needs        | Tracks scope and changes     | Refines user stories                 |
| Prioritization      | Business and customer value  | Delivery constraints         | Backlog priorities                   |
| Timeline            | Considers product milestones | Manages schedules            | Supports sprint planning             |
| Budget              | Considers business case      | Often manages project budget | Usually not responsible              |
| Success measurement | Product KPIs                 | Project delivery metrics     | Value delivered through backlog      |
