
# Delivery Lifecycle: The 10 Steps from Client Discovery to Release

The Delivery Lifecycle is the complete process of taking a client's idea, problem, or business requirement and turning it into a working product or feature that is delivered to the client or end users.

A Product Manager (PM) plays an important role throughout this lifecycle by understanding client needs, defining requirements, prioritizing features, coordinating stakeholders, and ensuring that the final product delivers value.

There are 10 defined steps in a Delivery Lifecycle, which include:

## 1. Client Discovery

Client Discovery is the first step in the delivery lifecycle. The purpose is to understand the client's business, objectives, challenges, and expectations before starting development.

The Product Manager or Business Analyst communicates with the client to understand:

- What does the client's business do?
- What problem are they trying to solve?
- Who are their target customers?
- What are their business objectives?
- What challenges are they currently facing?
- What are their expectations from the product?

## 2. Requirement Gathering

After understanding the client's business, the next step is to gather detailed requirements.

Requirement gathering means identifying exactly what the client expects the product to do. The PM or Business Analyst communicates with the client and relevant stakeholders to collect functional and non-functional requirements.

Functional requirements describe what the system should do, whereas non-functional requirements describe how the system should perform.

## 3. Requirement Analysis

After gathering requirements, the team analyzes them to determine whether they are clear, realistic, achievable, and valuable. The purpose is to identify possible issues before development begins.

For this, the team evaluates:

- **Feasibility:** Can we technically build this?
- **Business Value:** Does this feature solve a meaningful business problem?
- **Scope:** What exactly is included and excluded?
- **Dependencies:** Does one feature depend on another?
- **Risks:** What challenges might arise?
- **Prioritization:** Which requirements are most important?

## 4. Solution Planning

Once the requirements are understood and analyzed, the team decides how the product will be developed.

This step involves planning the technical and functional solution. In this process, the team determines:

- Overall system architecture.
- Technology stack.
- Database structure.
- Third-party integrations.
- APIs and system communication.
- Security considerations.
- Development approach.

## 5. Design and Prototyping

After solution planning, the UI/UX team starts designing how the product will look and how users will interact with it.

For this, the team takes the gathered information and creates wireframes and/or mockups. In this process, the created mockups or wireframes are continuously reviewed and verified with stakeholders, such as clients and other relevant team members, to ensure alignment and support a smoother development process going forward.

## 6. Development Planning

Before actual coding begins, the team plans how the development work will be organized and delivered. During this stage, the requirements and approved designs are broken down into smaller, manageable tasks.

The team creates an Agile Product Backlog, which contains all the features and tasks that need to be developed. These requirements are written as User Stories to describe what users need and why they need it, along with Acceptance Criteria to define the conditions that must be met for a feature to be considered complete.

The team then estimates the effort required for each task, assigns responsibilities, and organizes the work into Sprints (short development cycles). They also identify dependencies between tasks and establish important milestones to ensure that development progresses in a structured and organized manner.

## 7. Development

This is the stage where the actual product is built. During this phase, the development team converts the approved requirements and designs into functional software.

This involves Frontend Development, where developers build the user-facing part of the application, and Backend Development, where they implement the server-side logic and business functionalities.

The team also works on Database Implementation to store and manage application data, API Development to enable communication between different components of the system, and Third-Party Integrations to connect external services such as payment gateways or authentication providers.

Throughout development, developers conduct Code Reviews to maintain code quality and follow Version Control and Code Management practices to track changes, collaborate efficiently, and maintain different versions of the source code.

## 8. Testing and Quality Assurance

Once development is completed, the product goes through testing to ensure that it works as expected.

The QA team verifies that the software meets the defined requirements and identifies defects.

Common types of testing include:

- **Functional Testing:** Checks whether features work correctly.
- **Integration Testing:** Checks whether different components work together.
- **Performance Testing:** Checks system performance under different workloads.
- **Security Testing:** Identifies security weaknesses.
- **Regression Testing:** Ensures new changes haven't broken existing functionality.
- **User Acceptance Testing (UAT):** Validates whether the solution meets business requirements.

## 9. Client Review and Approval

After internal testing is completed, the product or feature is presented to the client for review and evaluation.

The purpose of this stage is to ensure that the developed solution meets the client's expectations and aligns with the previously agreed requirements.

During this phase, the development team demonstrates the completed features to the client, allowing them to review the functionality and provide feedback. The client and other stakeholders examine the product to identify any issues, missing requirements, or areas that need improvement.

Based on their feedback, the team discusses and agrees on any necessary changes or modifications. Once the client is satisfied that the solution meets the agreed requirements and acceptance criteria, they provide formal approval or sign-off, allowing the product to proceed towards release and deployment.

## 10. Release and Deployment

This is the final stage of the initial delivery lifecycle.

Once the product has successfully passed the required testing and client approval processes, it is deployed to the production environment, making it available to its intended users.

During this stage, the technical team prepares the production environment, deploys the application, and configures the necessary production settings to ensure everything works correctly.

If required, existing data is migrated to the new system, followed by final Smoke Testing to verify that the application's essential functionalities are working properly after deployment.

Once the product is live, the team continuously monitors its performance to identify unexpected errors, downtime, or other issues.

The launch is also communicated to relevant stakeholders to officially inform them that the product or feature is now available to users.
