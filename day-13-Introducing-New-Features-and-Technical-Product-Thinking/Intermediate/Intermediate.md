
# Client Pitching, Product Roadmaps, Technical Feasibility, and Non-Functional Requirements

These concepts help product and project teams propose features, plan development, evaluate technical solutions, and ensure product quality.

## 1. Client Pitching

### What Is Client Pitching?

Client pitching is the process of presenting a proposed feature or improvement to a client and explaining its business value.

The goal is to show how the proposed solution addresses a problem or benefits the business.

### What Should a Feature Pitch Include?

- **Problem:** What business problem needs to be solved?
- **Proposed solution:** What feature or improvement is being suggested?
- **Business value:** How will it benefit the business?
- **Effort and cost:** How much time and resources will it require?
- **Expected outcome:** What results should the client expect?

### What Is a Business Case?

A business case explains why a feature is worth investing in by comparing its development costs with its expected benefits.

Benefits may include increased revenue, reduced costs, improved efficiency, or better customer satisfaction.

## 2. Product Roadmaps: Now, Next, and Later

### What Is a Product Roadmap?

A product roadmap is a high-level plan that communicates a product's development direction, priorities, and future improvements.

It helps teams and clients understand what is being developed now and what may come next.

### Now, Next, and Later

| Stage | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Now** | Features currently being developed or prioritised. |
| **Next** | Features expected to be worked on after current priorities. |
| **Later** | Potential future features that are not yet scheduled or fully defined. |

### Keeping Roadmaps Realistic

A realistic roadmap should consider:

- Team capacity and available resources.
- Development and testing time.
- Technical dependencies and risks.
- Changing business priorities.
- Unexpected issues and delays.

**Important:** A roadmap communicates direction and priorities, not a guarantee that every feature will be delivered on a specific date.

## 3. Technical Feasibility

### What Is Technical Feasibility?

Technical feasibility determines whether a proposed feature can realistically be developed using available technology, resources, time, and technical capabilities.

Before committing to a feature, product teams should discuss its feasibility with engineers.

### What Should Be Considered?

- **Implementation options:** What technical approaches are available?
- **Development effort:** How much time and work will each approach require?
- **Dependencies:** Does the feature depend on existing systems, APIs, or external services?
- **Technical limitations:** Are there restrictions in the existing system?
- **Risks:** Could the solution create performance, security, or maintenance problems?

### What Are Technical Trade-offs?

Technical trade-offs involve choosing between different solutions, each with its own advantages and disadvantages.

For example, a simpler solution may cost less initially but be harder to maintain later. A more scalable solution may require additional time and development costs.

**Key point:** Product decisions should balance business value, development effort, and technical feasibility.

## 4. Non-Functional Requirements

### What Are Non-Functional Requirements?

Non-functional requirements describe how well a system should operate rather than what functions it should perform.

They define the expected quality, reliability, and technical characteristics of a product.

### Main Non-Functional Considerations

| Area | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Performance** | How quickly and efficiently the system responds. |
| **Security** | How the system protects data and prevents unauthorized access. |
| **Scalability** | How well the system handles increasing users, data, and activity. |
| **Maintainability** | How easily developers can fix, update, and improve the system. |
| **Maintenance Cost** | The ongoing cost of supporting and maintaining the system. |

### Why Are They Important?

A feature may work correctly but still create problems if it is slow, insecure, difficult to maintain, or unable to handle increased usage.

Considering these requirements early helps prevent technical problems and expensive improvements later.

## 5. Quick Comparison

| Topic | Main Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Client Pitching** | Explain the business value of a proposed feature. |
| **Product Roadmaps** | Communicate development priorities and future direction. |
| **Technical Feasibility** | Determine whether a feature can realistically be built. |
| **Non-Functional Requirements** | Ensure the product is reliable, secure, efficient, and maintainable. |
