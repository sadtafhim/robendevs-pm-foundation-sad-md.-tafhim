# Work Breakdown, Estimation, Dependencies, Sprint Planning, and Backlog Grooming

## 1. Work Breakdown

Work breakdown is the process of dividing a large feature into smaller tasks that can be assigned, estimated, and completed more easily.

Tasks are commonly divided across:
- **Design:** UI layouts, prototypes, and design specifications.
- **Frontend:** Building the user interface.
- **Backend:** Developing APIs, business logic, and database functionality.
- **QA:** Testing functionality and identifying issues.

Each task should have a clear title, description, expected outcome, and assigned owner. Tasks should be small enough to manage but not so small that they create unnecessary overhead.

## 2. Estimation

Estimation helps teams understand how much effort a task requires and how much work can fit into a sprint.

**Story Points** measure relative effort based on complexity, workload, and uncertainty. Common scales use 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, and 13 points.

Common estimation methods:

- **Planning Poker:** Team members independently estimate a task, compare their estimates, discuss differences, and agree on a final value.
- **T-shirt Sizing:** Tasks are classified as XS, S, M, L, or XL based on their relative size. This is useful for early or high-level planning.

Story points are not exact hours. They help teams compare tasks and plan consistently.

## 3. Dependencies

Dependencies occur when one task relies on another task being completed before work can continue.

For example, frontend development may depend on approved designs or completed backend APIs.

In Jira, issue links help track these relationships, such as:
- **Blocks:** One task prevents another from progressing.
- **Is blocked by:** A task depends on another task.
- **Relates to:** Two tasks are connected but do not directly depend on each other.

Identifying dependencies early helps teams avoid delays and plan work in the correct order.

## 4. Sprint Planning

Sprint planning determines what work the team will complete during an upcoming sprint.

The team considers:
- **Priority:** Which tasks are most important?
- **Capacity:** How much work can the team realistically handle?
- **Estimates:** How much effort does each task require?
- **Dependencies:** Are required tasks ready to begin?

The team selects suitable backlog items and defines a sprint goal. The selected work should match the team's available capacity.

Sprint scope is a forecast, not an unconditional promise. Changes may be necessary as work progresses, while keeping the sprint goal in focus.

## 5. Backlog Grooming

Backlog grooming, also called backlog refinement, keeps upcoming work clear, prioritised, and ready for development.

It typically involves:
- Reviewing and clarifying backlog items.
- Adding acceptance criteria.
- Breaking large tasks into smaller ones.
- Estimating effort.
- Identifying dependencies.
- Updating priorities.

A backlog item is generally ready when its requirements are clear, its scope is manageable, and the team understands what needs to be delivered.

Backlog refinement happens throughout the project, while sprint planning focuses on selecting work for the next sprint.
